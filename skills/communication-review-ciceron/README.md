# Communication Review Ciceron

Philosopher idea: Cicero's rhetoric becomes a review habit: judge a message or a screen by what it does to its audience, not only by what it says or shows.

Practical use: Use when a message, proposal, or copy may be misread, at final validation of UI-changing work, or to inspect a page, screenshot, or mockup. It separates observed wording and visible signals from inferred intent and attention, names likely audience uptake and friction, and gives the smallest correction: a better version of the message, or the change that points the screen's attention at its goal.

## What it does

- Separates observed wording from inferred intent and recommended revision.
- For messages, reviews goal, speech act, audience, emotional impact, rhetorical position, tension, scoring, and reformulation.
- For rendered screens, reviews in two passes, using Kahneman's System 1 and System 2 as a practical model: a first-glance read of what pops out, then an evidence check of that impression. This is the method of the former UI Attention Ciceron skill.
- Shows contested attention judgments in an Attention Evidence Matrix, a transparency tool rather than a score or an eye-tracking result.
- Gives straight feedback without pretending to read minds, and keeps necessary firmness, warnings, and accessibility affordances.

## Interference options for UI review

Choose the level per project or session:

- `Passive`: report findings only.
- `Advisory`: default. Small issues are optional polish, and a missed primary goal is a fix the work still needs.
- `Strict`: major attention conflicts block final completion unless the user explicitly waives them.
