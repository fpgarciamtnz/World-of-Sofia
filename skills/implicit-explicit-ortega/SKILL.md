# Implicit Explicit Ortega

Ortega y Gasset separated two ways of holding a thought. Ideas are explicit: we have them, state them, and argue about them. Beliefs, his *creencias*, are implicit: we stand on them without noticing, so nobody states them, and they show up only when they fail. "Las ideas se tienen; en las creencias se está" (*Ideas y creencias*, 1940). His late work on language adds that every saying is deficient, because it says less than it means, and exuberant, because it conveys more than it intends.

This skill makes both kinds of implicit content visible in a piece of work, then decides which to state, which to cut, and which to leave alone. It matters whenever the reader lacks the writer's circumstance: another agent, a model that follows instructions literally, a tutor, a teammate, or the user a month later. What was obvious to the writer is missing for that reader, and what the text implies beyond its intent becomes a rule they follow.

## Degree of freedom

`exploratory`. Look for beliefs everywhere, including in the user's request and in your own plan, since your own are the hardest to see. Report what you find before changing anything: edit the material only when the user asked for it or when the edit is part of the task at hand. Ask before writing a newly stated belief into a durable place the user did not ask to change, such as project instructions, memory, or shared docs, because a stated belief starts to govern.

## Three readings

Read the material three times, each for one thing:

- **Explicit:** what it actually says, and where. Quote it rather than paraphrasing it into something stronger or weaker.
- **Implicit by deficiency**, what it says less of than it means: what it relies on without saying. Look at the reader's context (which machine, project, branch, or earlier conversation), conventions taken as given ("as usual", "obviously", "the normal way"), terms used as if defined, defaults nobody chose, who decides, and what counts as done.
- **Implicit by exuberance**, what it conveys beyond its intent: what a reader will take from it that the writer did not mean. Look at examples that read as rules, emphasis that reads as priority, step order that reads as mandatory, a word that carries more weight in this field, and silence read as permission, as prohibition, or as "nothing to report".

## Finding beliefs

A belief is hard to see from inside, because nobody holds it as a thought. Find it by its traces:

- what everyone acts on and nobody wrote down;
- what would surprise the writer if it turned out false;
- cracks: a misunderstanding, a failed handoff, a "but I assumed", a check that broke for a reason nobody predicted. For Ortega a doubt is a hole in a belief, so where a doubt appears, a belief was holding;
- your own assumptions about the user, the environment, and the task.

## Deciding

Give each implicit item one decision and its reason:

- **State it** when the reader lacks the circumstance that makes it obvious, when something depends on it, or when it may be false. Write the exact sentence and where it goes.
- **Cut or reword** an exuberant implication the writer does not want.
- **Leave it implicit** when the reader shares the circumstance and stating it would only add noise.
- **Ask** when only the user knows which reading is meant: one question, with your recommended answer.

Stating everything is its own failure, since a text that spells out every belief buries the few that matter. State what changes what the reader will do.

## Output

- **Explicit:** what is said, with its location.
- **Implicit:** each item, marked as deficiency or exuberance, with the trace that shows it and its decision.
- **Changes:** the sentences to add, cut, or reword, ready to paste, when the material is text.

Read [references/examples.md](references/examples.md) when the material is of a new kind, or when you cannot tell whether an item is worth stating.

## Completion

The skill is finished when every implicit item found has a decision with its reason, every "state it" has its exact sentence and place, and every question only the user can answer has been asked.
